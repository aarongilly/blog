## The Productivity "Expert"

A person who's tried 50 different productivity apps is **not** a productivity expert. They are a productivity _app_ enthusiast, to be sure, but there's nothing _productive_ about switching productivity systems. Maintenance of the system that you use to do the stuff you find valuable is, itself, not the valuable thing. It's **overhead**. Some level of maintenance is necessary, to be sure, and it's often true that there's a better, more effective way that you just haven't tried yet... but at a certain point enough is enough. You're not likely to find some brand new tool or idea that genuinely moves the needle on productivity.[^3]

You know who gets crap done? People who: 

1) **Know what's important**
2) Have a system that gives them **the consistent best chance to progress those important things**

That's it. 

Having "the best productivity app" or "the ultimate productivity stack" or even a full dedication to some sort of organizational method that 

The most productive thing you can possibly do is maximize the amount of work **not done** while still achieving the result you're looking for.
